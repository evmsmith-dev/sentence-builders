# Sentence Builders

A grammar and punctuation game for grades 3–8 that installs as an app on phones, tablets and computers (PWA) and works offline. Players fix and build sentences; each level they pass puts up a building on Grammar Island, and badges reward mastery and steady practice. Skills follow the 2020 Minnesota K–12 ELA standards.

Six places on **Grammar Island** hold the skills, as worlds: **Punctuation Port**, **Word Works**, **Sentence Studio**, **Flow Factory** (Shurley-style Q&A Flow), **Verb Valley** (verbs, with grade 6–8 skills on verbals and parallel structure) and, for grades 6–8, **Revision Ridge**. The tables list each level's Tip Card and grade; they're built from `SKILLS` in `src/config.js` and the Tip Cards in `data/content.json`.

Flow Factory levels are 5 items: 3 Q&A Flows, then 2 Build-from-labels sentences, which must be right for Gold. In a flow, players answer one question at a time (tap the word, then pick its label), and a **🏷️ Labels** button opens a reminder of every label at that level (name, meaning, how to find it) at no cost; a miss gives a hint for that step, and a second miss shows that step and moves on. Labels and question order follow Shurley English Levels 3–8 as commonly taught; the sentences are original. Advanced Q&A Flow only appears for players in grade 6 or higher. Grades 3–5 Q&A Flow levels include commands with the understood subject (You), questions, sentences with not (an adverb) and inverted word order (Down the hill rolled the ball): in an inverted sentence the subject comes last, so its mark-up is the noun check and parentheses, without a slash. **Parts of Speech** (grades 3–5) asks the test-style question (what part of speech is this word here?): nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs; then all eight; then the same word doing different jobs. **Compound Q&A Flow** (grades 3–5) teaches Shurley's compound parts by grade, following Shurley's Level 3–5 label lists: level 1 compound subjects (CSN, CSP), verbs (CV), objects of the preposition (COP), the conjunction (C) and interjections (I); level 2 adds compound direct objects (CDO) and transitive verbs (CV-t); level 3 adds compound indirect objects (CIO), predicate nouns (CPrN), predicate adjectives (CPA), linking verbs (CLV), adjectives (CAdj), adverbs (CAdv) and predicate pronouns (PrP, CPrP). Advanced Q&A Flow level 3 uses every label. In the mark-up, compound nouns are circled, a prepositional phrase runs through its last compound object, and an interjection is in neither the subject nor the predicate. Diagrams (level 3) fork any compound: subjects, verbs (including a compound verb sharing one direct object), direct objects, predicate nouns, pronouns and adjectives on the baseline, and compound objects of a preposition or compound indirect objects where they hang; compound adjectives, adverbs and interjections aren't diagrammed yet.

Revision Ridge opens for players in grade 6 or higher. Minnesota's grade 6–8 benchmarks (6.2.1.1, 7.2.1.1, 8.2.1.1) ask for correct punctuation, capitalization and grammar in edited writing, so its skills follow the usual middle-school sequence. Proofreading levels are 5 paragraphs: tap a mistake, pick the fix.

**Revising Sentences** (Sentence Studio, grades 3–5): synonyms and antonyms; revising with a synonym, an antonym or a word change; then adding detail or deleting extra words.

**Sentence Combining** (Sentence Studio, grades 3–5) is the one skill where kids type: two short sentences become one, written out in full. Level 1 joins the parts that differ with and or or (Leo and Mia like pizza, so the verb changes too); level 2 makes compound sentences with a comma and and, but, so or or; level 3 uses because, when, although, if, after, before, while, since or until, with the joining part first or second. Every right way to write it is accepted (either order), contractions count the same as the words spelled out, and spaces don't matter. A first miss names the slip when the words are right: a missing capital, a capital that doesn't belong, the end mark, a comma, one misspelled word or the joining word to use (`checkCombine` in `src/lib/parse.js`). The phone's autocorrect and auto-capitals are off in the box so the capitals and commas are the child's own. Its badge is 🔨 Sentence Smith.

Word study and style (added to cover more of the 2020 Minnesota benchmarks): **Word Parts** (prefixes and suffixes, many from Old English; Latin roots; Greek roots: 3.1.1.1, 4.2.1.2, 5.2.1.2, 6.2.1.2), **Word Choice** (formal and informal; connotation; precise words: 4.2.3.2, 5.1.8.1, 6.2.3.2) and **Figurative Language** (similes; metaphors and idioms; personification and hyperbole: 3.1.8.1, 4.1.8.1, 5.2.6.1). Agreement level 1 includes collective nouns (team, class, flock: 3.2.1.3), and pronoun case level 2 includes intensive pronouns (I made it myself: 7.2.1.3). The Rule Book's word-part cards (`WORD_PARTS`) list common prefixes, suffixes and roots with their meanings.

## How it plays

- Up to 4 players per device, each with a name, avatar and grade. Levels at or below a player's grade start unlocked, so an older child can review anything.
- A level opens with a Tip Card (the rule and an example, with read-aloud), then 8 items: 6 on the skill and 2 review items from levels already passed (Flow Factory: 5 items, see above).
- Ten kinds of items: **Mark it** (tap a space, add the marks; where commas are the only choice, a tap adds or removes one), **Fix it** (find the wrong word and fix it), **Which is right?**, **Build it** (put the pieces in order) **Sort it** (tap a word, then the box it belongs in), **Sentence check** (complete, fragment or run-on, then the best fix) **Join it** (pick the joining word, then decide if a comma goes in), **Q&A Flow**, **Build from labels** and **Proofread**.
- A first miss shows a hint and allows a second try; a second miss shows the answer, and the item comes back later.
- Points: 1 for right on the first try, ½ on the second. Stars at 6, 7 and 8 points out of 8 (scaled for shorter levels); under 6 means "build it again", and nothing is lost.
- Gold on a skill: 7 or more on its level 3 on two different days. That raises a gold flag on its top building.
- Badges: one per skill (Bronze, Silver, Gold); one per world for every skill at Silver (Port Champion, Works Wizard, Studio Star, Factory Foreman, Chief Editor; only the skills open at the player's grade count); and habits: First Brick, Builder's Week, Fix-It Pro, Summit Climber (5 flags from Summit Reviews) and Spelling Sailor (5 boats from Spelling Lake).
- Settings per player: read every sentence aloud, Pip's comments, sounds, grade.
- **Looking back:** the results screen lists the round's mistakes ("Look again"), each written correctly with its rule, and a **Review the rule** button reopens that level's Tip Card and returns to the results. The **Rule Book** (home screen chip, or the 📘 button on a world) has a tab per place, named as on the island map, plus **📚 Reference**. A **Find a rule** box at the top searches every unlocked rule, word list, kind-of-noun and spelling card, and every Q&A Flow label (try comma, its, plural or CDO). The search box and tabs wrap and stay pinned at the top. In a place's tab, **Skills** come first: each folds to one line until tapped, then shows its unlocked rules with examples and read-aloud (plus labels and patterns in Flow Factory); locked rules show only their title. Every reference card has one home, the **Reference** tab: word lists (articles, prepositions, helping and linking verbs, pronouns, joining words), kinds of nouns, word parts (prefixes, suffixes, Latin and Greek roots), and spelling patterns. Word Works and Flow Factory end with a link to it.
- **Back button:** the phone's Back gesture (or the browser's Back) closes the popup on top or steps back one screen, as in Bible Trivia and World Geography. Mid-round it asks before leaving; on the results it goes to the island; on the island it leaves the app.
- **📊 Progress** (on the home screen and on Who's playing?, open to everyone), like Bible Trivia and World Geography: **Me** has the current player's full details (minutes and days played this week, stars and badge for every skill, and the sentences missed most); **Everyone** (2+ players) has a short card each: buildings built, badges and minutes this week. From Who's playing, with no one picked, it shows Everyone.
- **📄 Teacher report** (Progress → Me): a one-page report for the current player to print, save as a PDF (the device's print screen offers Save as PDF or Save to Files) or share as text (the phone's share sheet, or copied when there isn't one). It covers the last 7 days, 30 days or all time: minutes, days played and levels passed in that range; an optional note from home; **Needs practice** (up to five skills under 75% right on the first try, with 6+ answers, each with two missed sentences and their answers); Shurley-style Q&A Flow levels passed; and every skill started so far with its Minnesota benchmarks, levels and stars, badge and first-try rate (skills not started are listed by name only). Levels record the day they were first passed (`passedOn`) for the ranges; levels passed before that are counted only in All time. Built by `teacherReport` and `reportText` in `src/lib/report.js`; nothing leaves the device unless it's printed or shared.
- **Settings** (⚙️): read aloud, comments and greeting, sounds, look, reading support, names in sentences, and a break reminder (off, 15, 20, 30 or 45 minutes) for the current player, plus saving and restoring a backup file of every player on the device, and ℹ️ About this game (also on Who's playing?): how to play, the Minnesota standards and Shurley English basis, and privacy. Switch players by tapping your name; edit a player with ✏️ on their card.
- Everything is saved on the device (localStorage). No accounts, ads, tracking or network calls.

## Development

```sh
npm install
npm start               # play the source at http://localhost:8080
npm test                # check the items, unit tests, then build and run the browser tests
npm run preview         # build and serve dist/ at http://localhost:8080
```

To run the browser tests with an installed Chrome instead of Playwright's download, set `CHROME_PATH` (for example `CHROME_PATH=/usr/bin/google-chrome npm test`).

Every browser test runs on the phone size; desktop and tablet run only the tests tagged `@layout` (the map, dragging, sideways overflow, the Rule Book's tabs), since game logic behaves the same at every size. Tag a new test `{ tag: "@layout" }` if it checks how things fit or are tapped. `ALL_SIZES=1 npm test` runs every test at every size.

Pushes to `main` are tested and deployed to GitHub Pages by `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`. The browser tests run on four machines at once (the phone tests split in two, plus desktop and tablet) with the Chrome already installed on GitHub's runners; pushes that only change Markdown files skip the run. One-time setup: **Settings → Pages → Build and deployment → Source: GitHub Actions**. The site is then at `https://evmsmith-dev.github.io/sentence-builders/`.

The Fredoka, Atkinson Hyperlegible and OpenDyslexic fonts (SIL Open Font License) and canvas-confetti (ISC) are stored in `vendor/` so the game works offline.

## Content

Mark it supports `-`, `—`, `…`, `(` and `)`; hyphens and dashes join words with no spaces (write `twenty[-]one`). About 16–20 items per level, 12 or more Q&A Flows per Flow Factory level, 20 paragraphs per Proofreading level: 1780 items in all, 60 weeks of Sentence of the Week, plus 74 homework paragraphs and 90 Spelling Lake questions. `data/README.md` explains how items are written.

## Mark it up and Sentence Diagramming

After every Q&A Flow, **Mark it up** walks through the Shurley Skill Check on the same sentence: circle the nouns (noun check), put parentheses around each prepositional phrase, go back to the verb and divide the complete subject from the complete predicate with a slash (directly before the verb and its helping verb), then underline the complete subject once and the complete predicate twice. Predicate words in the complete subject (a helping or linking verb that starts a question, an adverb, or a prepositional phrase at the front) are underlined twice. On the first Q&A Flow sentence of each level (grade 4+), Shurley's **noun table** follows: for each noun, its job (SN, OP, DO…), singular or plural, and common or proper, then the simple subject and simple predicate. Pronouns doing a noun's job (me, us) aren't circled or listed. Each question shows a one-line reminder until the player has 12 right on the first try; 🏷️ Nouns (in the step) and a Kinds of nouns card in the Rule Book's Reference tab explain common/proper and singular/plural. Word-ending cards (`SPELLING_RULES`: past tense -ed, irregular past tense verbs, plurals, future tense with will, -s and -ing) fold in the Rule Book's Reference tab. Singular/plural comes from the reviewed noun list in `src/lib/nouns.js`; the item checker stops any new sentence whose nouns aren't on it. The flow itself asks for the sentence type (D, E, Int, Imp) and the pattern (SN V P1, …; levels 1–2 ask Pattern 1 or 2). The marks come from the word labels (`src/lib/markup.js`), so no extra content is needed.

**Sentence Diagramming** (Flow Factory, grade 5+, levels for grades 5/6/7, 5 sentences a level) starts with the same mark-up, then the player places each word on a Reed-Kellogg diagram drawn from the labels (`src/lib/diagram.js`): subject | verb on the baseline, direct objects behind a short line, predicate words and object complements behind a leaning line, describing words on slanted lines, prepositional phrases and indirect objects hanging below. The first diagram of each level is guided (one line at a time, with why the word goes there); **Walk me through it** replays that on any diagram (half credit), and every finished diagram lists why each word goes where it does. A word like *very* that describes a describing word hangs on its own slanted line joined to that word's line (an adverb before another adverb only does this for intensifiers like very and really; `attach` in an item overrides the guess). Compound parts (taught in Compound Q&A Flow, Advanced Q&A Flow level 3 and Diagramming level 3) fork into two branches with the conjunction on a dashed line: subjects, verbs (a compound verb can share one direct object), direct objects, predicate nouns, pronouns and adjectives on the baseline, and compound objects of a preposition or compound indirect objects where they hang. The two parts may go on either branch. Not yet diagrammed: three or more compound parts, compound adjectives and adverbs, and interjections.

## Listen and Write and Sentence of the Week

- **🎧 Listen and Write** (Punctuation Port, grades 3–5; badge 👂 Sharp Ears): dictation. Pip reads a sentence aloud with the device's voice (it starts on its own; 🔊 Listen, 🧩 Phrase by phrase, which reads it whole, in phrases, then whole again, and 🐢 Slowly, as often as the child likes), and the child types it. The typed sentence is lined up with the real one word by word (`checkDictation` in `src/lib/parse.js`): capitals, end marks, commas, quotation marks and apostrophes count, and the first slip is named in the hint ("Add a comma after said", "Duluth needs a capital letter"); a missing or different word counts too. A misspelled word doesn't (two swapped letters count as one slip), nor does a sound-alike word: those are listed after the answer to practice. Because a voice can't show an exclamation point, a period counts for one. Names can sound alike (Mia, Maya), so any spelling of a name counts, as long as it has its capital; the name's spelling is shown after the answer. Settings → **Listen and Write: show the words too** shows the sentence in small letters without marks (for hearing or listening difficulties), and a device without a voice always shows them. Levels follow the teaching expert's sequence: capitals, end marks, lists, dates and simple dialogue (grade 3); compound-sentence commas, ? and ! inside quotation marks, contractions and possessives (grade 4); commas after opening words and clauses, direct address and split quotations (grade 5). 48 original sentences.
- **📌 Sentence of the Week** (the Notice Board in the Town Square, and the first row of the **Today with Pip** card on the home screen): one original sentence for the week, different for grades 3–4, 5 and 6–8 (`weeks` in `data/content.json`, 20 per band, taking turns week by week, the same for everyone in a band; a week already started keeps its sentence even if new weeks are added partway through it). Each weekday has a two-minute job, analysis before editing as in daily grammar practice: **Monday** parts of speech (two words), **Tuesday** the Q&A Flow, **Wednesday** the mark-up (Skill Check), **Thursday** fixing it (the sentence written with 2–4 mistakes: capitals, verbs, end marks, a pronoun-case trap in grades 6–8), **Friday** typing it joined to another sentence (and, but, so, because, when, while…). Days open on their weekday, and a missed day can be caught up any time before Monday (all five are open on the weekend); nothing is lost when a new week starts. It's optional, like Pip's homework: each answer earns bricks, and finishing all five earns 15 more and hangs a pennant banner in the Town Square (the latest 12 show; hover one for its sentence). The Notice Board shows the five days stamped as they're done and a red ! when a job is waiting; tapping it (or the Sentence of the Week row in Today with Pip) opens the week: the sentence posted in small letters without marks, each day's job and Start buttons. The week's sentence isn't changed to the player's names, so it stays the same all week. `src/lib/week.js` builds each day's items from the week's sentence, labels and steps; the content checker makes sure Thursday's fixed text is the week's sentence and every day's items can be played.
- **Today with Pip** (home screen): Sentence of the Week, Pip's homework and Build on the island as three compact rows of one card (a title and one short line each), so the home screen doesn't grow a card per feature.

## The island plan (grades 6–8)

Following the teaching and UI experts, players in grade 6 and up see Grammar Island as an architect's **blueprint** instead of the cartoon island: the same map, paths and buildings drawn as pale outlines on grid paper, with passed levels as outline buildings and levels still to go as dashed footprints. Each skill's label shows how many of its levels are built and its first-try rate (Commas · 2/3 · 85%), in gold when it's under 75%, so weak spots show instead of hiding behind buildings. There are no decorations, no Pip on the map and no brick shop: the Build row and Decorate here are hidden, and bricks aren't mentioned anywhere (results, the Notice Board, Pip's welcome, Sentence of the Week, whose reward is the week's banner on the plan), though they still count up quietly for anyone who turns the colorful island back on, and Grammar Peaks is labeled Review. On the home page the **Worlds list comes first**, and the plan below it is a short preview with one line ("12 of 96 levels built · 2 skills under 75% · 9 to review"); tapping it (or Open the plan) opens the full pan-and-zoom plan. Settings → **Show Grammar Island in color, with decorations and building** (off by default, shown only from grade 6) brings the regular island back for anyone who likes it. It's one map drawn in a second style (`blueprint` in `townSvg`, the `.town.blueprint` styles), not a second screen.

## Pip for grades 6–8

Following the experts, Pip stays the mascot for every grade (he's on the app icon), but grades 6–8 meet him as a coworker rather than a buddy: a small line drawing instead of the colorful robot, no greeting on the home page (just "Next up" and ▶ Play, and the card is titled "Today"), and plainer words (`PIP_GROWN`: "Correct.", "Not yet. Hint:", "Level passed.", results titled Passed or Perfect score). His main job there is Pip's homework, as a slightly overconfident writer who asks to be graded honestly ("A C. In my defense, some of those were sneaky."). He still presents Tip Cards and sits beside hints, and stays off the map. Settings → Comments and greeting turns off his face, greetings and remarks for any grade; hints, homework and Tip Cards keep working in a plain voice.

## Challenge Trails (grades 3–5)

Following the teaching and game-design experts (without their one-grade-ahead limit, at the parent's request), levels above a 3rd–5th grader's grade open by mastery instead of by grade:
- **A skill's next level** opens once the level before it is scored 7 or 8 of 8 on two different days (`strongOn` on each level record; levels saved earlier count with 2+ stars and 2+ plays). There's no limit on how far ahead a strong student can go.
- **Skills that wait for a grade** open early once the skills they build on are Gold (`CHALLENGE_PREREQS`): Sentence Diagramming after Q&A Flow and Compound Q&A Flow; Advanced Q&A Flow after those and Diagramming; each Revision Ridge skill after its groundwork (Pronoun Case after Pronouns, Proofreading after Commas and Capitals, Dashes after Commas, Sentence Types after Compound and Complex, Modifiers after Complex). Revision Ridge's world opens with its first opened skill.
- **One new trail a day**, so it can't become a race; Pip announces it on the home page ("You got Commas and colons level 1 right on two different days, so you opened a Challenge Trail…") with a Try it button. Each skill card says what opens the next one.
- **Review first:** with missed sentences waiting (and no Summit Review yet today), a Challenge level shows Pip's "Review first!" with a Review now button.
- **No grade numbers for the kids:** those levels say ⭐ Challenge instead of a grade; the teacher report shows the grade with "Challenge".
- **For parents:** Settings → Levels above my grade (press and hold for a moment to change): Challenge Trails (the default), Only levels for my grade and below, or All levels open. Grades 6–8 are unchanged.

## Reading support and names in sentences

- **Reading support** (Settings, per player): **Reading font** switches the whole game to Atkinson Hyperlegible (made by the Braille Institute for clear letter shapes) or OpenDyslexic; **Text size** sets Large (112.5%) or Larger (125%), since every size in the stylesheet is in rem; **Extra space between letters and lines** widens letter, word and line spacing. All three apply as soon as they're picked.
- **Names in sentences** (Settings, off by default): the player's own first name, up to three family members or friends, each marked he or she, and a pet's name. About half the sentences that use one of the game's story names (`STORY_NAMES` in `src/lib/names.js`: Leo, Ben, Omar, Kai, Sam and Max are he; Mia, Ana, Rosa, Ava, Lily, Jada and Maya are she; Biscuit and Peanut are pets) swap it for one of theirs of the same kind, so his, her and himself still fit. The swap covers every part of the item at once (the sentence, the choices, the labels, the answers and the hint), including Maya's and the misspelled choices in Capital letters (maya, MAYA); a pet named in the sentence (a puppy named Max) keeps a pet's name, a name already in the sentence isn't added again, and names ending in s skip sentences with Maya's-style endings. A name must be one word, letters only, and not a word the game labels (bear, it); the noun table counts each one as a singular proper noun. Item history still uses each item's own id.

## Original content

Sentence Builders is a practice supplement for skills students have already been taught, or are assumed to have been taught, in class. Every sentence, hint, Tip Card and explanation is written for this game. It follows the Shurley English method (the Q&A Flow, its label abbreviations, the sentence patterns and the Skill Check steps) and the 2020 Minnesota standards, but copies no text, examples or jingles from Shurley's books or any other curriculum, and isn't made by or connected to Shurley Instructional Materials or the Minnesota Department of Education. The app icon and drawings are original too; the decorations are the device's own emoji.

**Sentence Workshop (Flow Factory, grades 3–5), recommended by the expert panel:** instead of labeling Pip's sentences, kids build their own. Each item gives a goal (Pattern 1 with an adjective and an adverb; Pattern 3 with a possessive pronoun…) and 10–13 word tiles, more than the sentence needs. Tiles can be tapped (added at the end, or tapped again to take out) or dragged: drag one between two words, move it within the sentence, or drop it back on the tiles to take it out. With a mouse or pen the drag starts when the pointer moves; on a touchscreen a tile lifts after a quarter-second press-and-hold, so a quick swipe across the tiles still scrolls the page. The lifted tile floats just above the finger and a bright line shows where it will land (`tileDrag` in `src/ui/items.js`, also used by Build a sentence). Asked for by our 4th grader for fitting a word in between placed tiles. Articles and possessives (the, a, an, my, his, her, our, their, your) stay in the bank with a ↻ mark and dashed border and can be used as many times as needed, so a child can put "the" before every noun; the expert panel found that one-of-each articles made most level 2 and Pattern 3 items a hidden puzzle (every article had to go in exactly one place) and left Pip suggesting "the" after it was used. The checker looks at the structure, not the exact words (`checkWorkshop` in `src/lib/workshop.js`), so "The sleepy puppy napped outside." and "A brown kitten wiggled happily." are both right; it capitalizes the first word and adds the period. A wrong build gets Pip's note on what to fix (no subject, the wrong kind of verb for the pattern, a singular noun without the/a/my, a before a vowel sound, was with a plural subject, a missing adverb or phrase), and Check doesn't end the item; after two misses "Use Pip's sentence" lets a stuck child go on. A right build goes straight to the Q&A Flow for that very sentence, with Shurley's questions written for it (`workshopFlowItem`) and played by the regular Q&A Flow screen, mark-up included. Then **📓 Save to my notebook** keeps it in Progress → Me → My notebook (newest first, up to 100). Following the earlier advice about one instruction, the goal in words sits in the card with 📘 and 🔊, and only its short form (GOAL SN V-t IO DO + Adj) stays pinned at the top with the sentence being built while the tiles scroll under it, the same sticky pattern as Q&A Flow; Pip's hint stays pinned at the bottom. Levels are 4 sentences. Item format: `{"type": "workshop", "pattern": 2, "need": ["Adj"], "tiles": "the|A hungry|Adj puppy|N chased|Vt napped|V ball|N …", "example": "the hungry puppy chased a ball"}` (kinds A, PPA, Adj, N, Np for a person, V, Vt, LV, Adv, P). The content checker runs each item's example through the checker and the Q&A Flow validator, and a unit test builds thousands of random sentences from every item's tiles and checks that each right one makes a valid Q&A Flow. 45 original items and 3 Tip Cards.
